How to Use AI for Technical Writing Without Publishing AI Slop

AI can speed up technical writing, but it should not become the source of truth. The writer remains responsible for evidence, accuracy, structure and the final document.

September 19, 2026Long form guide
← Back to all articles

Keep the writer responsible

Use AI for transformation and exploration: outlines, alternative wording, question generation, summaries of supplied material and controlled rewrites. Do not treat generated prose as proof of product behaviour, API details or policy. Fluency is not evidence.

Build a source pack

Collect requirements, approved terminology, implementation notes, reference material, examples, release notes and decisions before drafting. Identify which source is authoritative when documents disagree. The source pack gives the writing process a boundary.

Use AI for structure first

Ask for possible outlines before asking for a complete document. Review the outline against the reader's job. Move prerequisites before dependent steps and give common failure paths enough space. The writer chooses the information architecture.

Draft in controlled pieces

Large one-shot prompts encourage generic prose and make verification difficult. Draft sections with a defined purpose, audience, source boundary and constraints. If information is missing, ask the model to flag the gap instead of filling it with a plausible invention.

Verify consequential claims

Check commands, parameter names, endpoints, versions, limits, authentication instructions, security statements and anything else where an error could cause real harm or wasted time. Verification should happen against the real product or an authoritative source.

Remove generic prose

Delete introductions that could describe any product. Cut repetition. Replace vague benefits with concrete behaviour. Turn abstract advice into examples. Good editing is not about hiding that AI was used; it is about making the final document useful and specific.

Test examples

A code example is a technical claim. Verify it. Ask whether a reader can use it without inventing missing values. Include realistic responses and failure cases where they help the task.

Reader testing

Give the finished draft to someone who was not involved in writing it. Watch where they hesitate. Do not rescue them during the test. Their confusion reveals missing prerequisites, unclear terminology, incomplete examples and bad information architecture.

Protect sensitive information

Technical writing can contain credentials, customer data, internal architecture and unreleased features. Follow the approved data handling rules for the AI system being used. Redact secrets and do not use real credentials as examples.

Separate reviews

First check factual correctness and completeness. Then check task flow, examples and failure paths. Only after that should you spend time on copy editing. A polished sentence cannot compensate for a missing instruction.

Final anti-slop checklist

Can every important claim be traced to a source? Are examples current and complete? Have invented details been removed? Is the document specific to a real reader task? Has someone else tested it? If the answer is yes, AI has served the writing process without taking control of it.
