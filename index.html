ABOUT

I sit between technical understanding and careful communication.

Why this portfolio looks different

AI quality work is not only about spotting wrong answers. It involves understanding the task, checking evidence, recognizing unsupported inference, comparing alternatives, and writing a rationale another reviewer can defend. My software engineering and cybersecurity background gives me a practical foundation for working with technical material rather than evaluating it only at the surface.

“I want the reasoning behind a quality judgment to be as clear as the judgment itself.”

That same principle carries into documentation, annotation, research, creative writing, and administrative work: understand the objective, inspect the details, and leave behind something another person can use.

AI QUALITY WORK

The portfolio evidence is organised around the same working habits required in model evaluation roles.

01
Prompt and task designBreak a requested outcome into clear criteria, constraints and testable behaviours before reviewing a response.
02
Grounding checksTrace claims about source material or user context back to available evidence and flag unsupported inference.
03
Side by side evaluationCompare responses against the same criteria rather than rewarding length, confidence or surface polish.
04
Defensible rationalesWrite concise explanations that identify the exact turn, behaviour, evidence and user impact behind a judgment.
05
Personalization qualityDistinguish useful context from forced connections, overnarration and claims that go beyond the evidence.
06
Data disciplineFollow project rules for context, annotation records, evaluation conversations and information handling.
Technical knowledge helps me understand the systems. Careful reading helps me evaluate what the systems produce.

HOW I WORK

A practical workflow for AI evaluation, documentation and data quality tasks.

01
Understand the taskIdentify the requested outcome, constraints, audience, evidence requirements and evaluation criteria before judging an output.
02
Inspect the evidenceSeparate what is supported from what is plausible, inferred, incomplete or contradicted.
03
Record the reasonWrite concise, specific rationales that point to the exact response, field, label or instruction that caused the judgment.
04
Improve the processUse repeated errors and disagreements to identify unclear guidelines, missing examples or workflow improvements.
The goal is not to make every output sound impressive. The goal is to make the quality of the work inspectable.
